“Interaction models” in plain English

The post argues that the next big interface shift is not just smarter autonomous agents. It is AI that can stay present with you: listening, watching, speaking, thinking, using tools, and adapting at the same time.

The tweet: “People talk, listen, watch, think, and collaborate at the same time, in real time. We’ve designed an AI that works with people the same way.”

The bottleneck they’re attacking

Turn-taking vs. real collaboration

Most chat/voice AI today

1User finishes a turn before the model really processes the full request.
2Model generates while perception is effectively frozen.
3Interruptions, visual cues, timing, and tool work are handled by external “harness” glue.

Interaction-model idea

1Audio, video, and text stream in continuously.
2The model can produce output while still listening or watching.
3Timing, interjections, silence, and tool updates become model behavior, not UI plumbing.

What becomes possible

Tap a topic
Seamless dialog managementThe model infers whether you are thinking, yielding, self-correcting, or inviting a response — without a separate turn detector.
Verbal + visual interjectionsIt can jump in when context demands: “you just wrote a bug,” “that rep counted,” or “that pronunciation was off.”
Simultaneous speechUser and model can overlap, which matters for live translation, coaching, and backchanneling.
Time-awarenessThe model has a direct sense of elapsed time, enabling reminders, pacing, and timed coaching.
Concurrent tool workIt can browse, search, or generate UI while continuing the conversation.
Continuous collaborationThe experience aims to feel less like prompting and more like working side-by-side.

Interaction model

Always present. Handles live audio/video/text, 200ms micro-turns, timing, interruptions, and immediate responses.

⇄

Background model

Runs asynchronously for deeper reasoning, tool use, search, and longer-horizon work. Streams results back when useful.

Early fusion

Less encoder scaffolding

Audio and image inputs are fed with minimal preprocessing and co-trained with the transformer rather than bolted on as separate systems.

Streaming sessions

Persistent GPU sequence

Each 200ms request appends to an existing sequence, avoiding repeated memory allocation and metadata overhead.

Why it matters

My read

The bet: interactivity should scale with intelligence.

If interaction is just a wrapper, smarter base models do not automatically become better collaborators. Thinking Machines is trying to make collaboration itself part of the model’s training and serving loop. That would make “AI teammate” less about a long-running agent you send away, and more about a live partner you can interrupt, show things to, talk over, and steer moment by moment.

Caveat: the post is a research preview, not a general product launch. The most interesting claims are qualitative capabilities that existing benchmarks do not fully capture yet.
