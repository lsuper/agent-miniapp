Murata now looks like an AI infrastructure winner — but the stock already trades like the market believes it.

Version 2 adds the two follow-ups: a valuation / what-to-watch-next view and a Murata-vs-TDK-vs-Taiyo Yuden comparison card, on top of the original bull/bear and deep operating breakdown from Murata’s Apr 30, 2026 FY2025 earnings release.

New follow-up #1Valuation lens

Murata’s Tokyo shares are only about 4% below their 52-week high. On current market data, the name screens at roughly 34x forward earnings and 5.4x book — not outrageous versus a re-rated AI winner, but no longer cheap for a company still carrying smartphone-exposed weak spots.

New follow-up #2Peer card

Versus Japanese passive/component peers, Murata trades at a much richer P/B than TDK, about the same forward P/E as TDK, and a meaningfully lower forward multiple than Taiyo Yuden. That suggests investors are pricing Murata as a quality AI capacitor winner, not just a generic handset-components supplier.

Investor view

Tap between the original earnings views and the 2 new follow-up sections.

3-point bull case

  • AI infrastructure is now the earnings engine. Capacitors for servers/data centers were the core offset to smartphone weakness, and management guided FY2026 revenue to ¥1.96T (+7.1%) with operating profit at ¥380B (+34.8%).
  • The beat was real, not just spin. FY2025 revenue beat February guidance by ¥30.9B, operating profit by ¥11.8B, pretax by ¥14.6B, and net profit by ¥13.9B.
  • Capital allocation turned friendlier. Murata raised the FY2025 dividend to ¥65/share, guided FY2026 to ¥70/share, and announced a ¥150B buyback, its largest ever.

What bulls are underwriting

  • Server-related capacitor sales in FY2026 up 85%–90%.
  • Additional data-center production investment of about ¥80B across FY2026–FY2027.
  • Computers/application bucket becoming increasingly data-center-driven rather than PC-driven.
  • Operating leverage finally showing up as utilization rises.
Most important unusual signal: Murata is no longer narrating itself mainly through handsets; the earnings deck is centered on data-center demand, capacitor mix, and power products.

3-point bear case

  • The “good” is concentrated. Smartphone-linked businesses are still weak: high-frequency modules and multilayer resin substrates declined, and FY2026 communication revenue is still guided down 2.2%.
  • FY2025 quality was less impressive than headline revenue. Revenue rose 5.0%, but operating profit only rose 0.8%; one-time charges included ¥43.8B of SAW-filter goodwill impairment.
  • Execution risk is still present. Murata disclosed a cyber incident and said secured hyperscaler-related secondary module orders were later dropped because of a firmware defect.

What bears are watching

  • Whether AI/data-center strength broadens beyond capacitors into durable multi-product growth.
  • Whether price declines and higher fixed costs again absorb much of the revenue upside.
  • Whether inventory build is healthy pre-positioning or a future digestion risk.
  • Whether smartphone recovery remains too weak to support the non-AI parts of the portfolio.
Most important caution flag: the market may be paying up for AI-exposure before proof that the broader portfolio has structurally improved.

What actually drove FY2025

  • Capacitors: ¥936.4B, +12.6% — the centerpiece of the story.
  • Inductors & EMI filters: ¥223.3B, +11.0% — helped by smartphones, mobility, and servers.
  • High-frequency device & communications modules: ¥394.8B, -11.0% — smartphone/PC drag.
  • Battery & power supply: ¥154.1B, -1.1% — mixed, with server power up but batteries down.
  • Functional devices: ¥107.1B, +9.5%.

Application mix read-through

  • Communication: ¥653.0B, -3.1% — still pressured by smartphone-linked substrate demand.
  • Mobility: ¥474.5B, +4.8% — steady support from AD/ADAS and xEVs.
  • Computers: ¥310.4B, +28.4% — strongest visible AI/data-center beneficiary.
  • Industry & others: ¥250.3B, +7.8%.
  • Home electronics: flat to modest.

Price reaction

  • 6981.T closed +6.5% on release day and about +63.8% above the pre-release close by May 25.
  • The market reaction says investors focused much more on FY2026 AI-capex / demand acceleration than on FY2025 profit quality.

Decision lens

  • If you are bullish: the right thesis is “Murata is becoming a picks-and-shovels AI infra beneficiary.”
  • If you are cautious: ask how much of today’s valuation already prices in multi-year data-center upside.
  • Most important next check: whether the next report confirms that AI/server strength is broadening while smartphone drag stops worsening.

Valuation read

  • Forward P/E ~34x: Murata is no longer screening as a neglected handset supplier; the market is paying an AI-infra winner multiple.
  • P/B ~5.4x: richer than TDK’s roughly 3.1x, implying a quality / mix premium.
  • Only ~4% below 52-week high: sentiment already reflects a good part of the earnings re-rating.
  • Dividend yield only 0.88%: the story is growth plus capital allocation, not income support.

What to watch next

  • Jul 30 earnings: current Yahoo Finance calendar points to Murata’s next earnings date on 2026-07-30.
  • Server capacitor growth durability: does management still point to ~85%–90% FY2026 server-capacitor growth?
  • Communication stabilization: does smartphone/high-frequency weakness stop getting worse?
  • Margins vs mix: does AI strength finally convert into broader operating leverage rather than getting offset by price declines and fixed costs?
  • Book-to-bill / inventory: Q4 book-to-bill was 1.24; the next report needs to show healthy demand rather than channel digestion risk.

Bullish valuation case

  • If Murata really has become a long-duration AI/passives beneficiary, then a premium multiple is rational because capacity, customer qualification, and product reliability create moats.
  • The buyback plus dividend step-up tells the market management also sees balance-sheet flexibility.
  • If July confirms broadening demand, investors may further rotate from smartphone skepticism into AI-platform quality.

Cautious valuation case

  • At ~34x forward earnings and near the 52-week high, Murata has less room for execution disappointment than it did pre-release.
  • The stock likely needs continued proof that AI/server demand is not just one pocket temporarily masking handset softness.
  • If communication or power businesses wobble again, the “premium quality AI compounder” framing could compress quickly.

What the peer card says

  • Murata vs TDK: basically the same forward earnings multiple (34.0x vs 33.9x), but Murata carries a much higher book multiple (5.4x vs 3.1x).
  • Murata vs Taiyo Yuden: Murata is still expensive, but less stretched than Taiyo Yuden’s 61.1x forward P/E.
  • Quality nuance: TDK currently shows the best ROE of the three, while Murata’s valuation premium is mostly riding on the AI-capacitor narrative and scale.

How I’d frame it

  • Best relative read: Murata looks like a premium-quality AI/passives name, not a bargain.
  • Most important peer benchmark: TDK is the one to watch, because Murata is no longer clearly cheaper on earnings multiples.
  • If Murata keeps beating on AI demand: its premium can hold.
  • If growth broadens at TDK faster or Murata’s smartphone drag lingers: the relative valuation gap can become harder to defend.
